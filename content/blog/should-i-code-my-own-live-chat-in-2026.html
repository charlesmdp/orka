Yes, you can build it. Should you own it?

Code your own live chat if the conversation experience is a core part of your product, you need control a hosted tool cannot give you, or the build itself is the point. If you mainly need to support customers across several products, compare the whole operating job before starting. A working widget is a useful milestone. It is only one part of a support platform.

You are a strong vibe coder. You can read a migration, inspect an agent’s diff, diagnose a websocket reconnect and ship a decent interface. This article assumes that competence. “You could never build this” would be a silly argument in 2026.

A better question is: which parts do you want to be responsible for next month? The missed message, the email that never arrived, the Android push token that expired, the search result from another project, the AI answer that ignored a handoff request?

Our interest is explicit.

The $0 rows are real. So are their limits.

You do not need a paid SaaS subscription for a saved reply, an emoji picker or a business-hours switch. Those features use code and the database you already run. That makes the incremental vendor bill small, while leaving the implementation and maintenance work intact.

Free allowances also belong to an account or project, depending on the service. If your existing apps have already used them, a new chat is not starting from zero. Separate staging environments, load tests and backups can consume the same allowance.

Real time can be cheap. Keeping everything awake can be expensive.

A good Cloudflare implementation can keep socket costs low using Durable Object hibernation. Idle connections remain open while the object is allowed to sleep; state must survive its reinitialization. Timers or continuously active work can prevent the expected savings. This is an architecture decision, not a discount code. Cloudflare’s hibernation guidance ↗

Track connection establishments, incoming frames, fan-out, active object duration and persisted writes separately. “We sent 10,000 chat messages” does not tell you any of those numbers. Typing previews, presence updates, tabs left open overnight and every teammate watching the inbox are part of the workload.

The same distinction applies to the map. A visitor moving between pages should update a pin or list entry, not recreate the map and buy another IP lookup. Cache suitable location data, expire presence, and update only what changed.

Five replies per conversation means five calls. Add a translation on each side, a summary, an urgency classifier, a retry and a confidence check, and “one conversation” contains many billable operations. Returning a cached FAQ answer can reduce calls. Blindly resending the complete history forever increases them.

Do not forget ingestion: fetching your site, parsing PDFs, making chunks, embedding new content and invalidating old vectors. Our scenario budgets use self-managed retrieval; OpenAI hosted file search is shown as an alternative in the ledger. A ChatGPT, Claude or coding-editor subscription is not a credit balance for your customer-facing bot.

The expenses that do not fit neatly into a per-message price.

  • Maintenance: reserve 4–12 hours/month for a small production installation, or 12–30 hours/month for the full suite with native apps and AI. These are our planning allowances; an incident or OS migration can exceed them. At $50/hour, that is $200–600 or $600–1,500 of time.
  • Hardware: $0 incremental if you already have suitable build access and test devices. Otherwise put an explicit $500–2,000 one-time planning allowance in your worksheet, then replace it with actual hardware quotes. This is a budget range, not a vendor price.
  • Coding agents: $0 incremental if your existing allowance covers the work; otherwise reserve $20–200/month as a planning range, then insert your actual tool’s subscription and usage. API overage can exceed it.
  • Domains and monitoring: an existing subdomain costs no additional registration. For a new domain, budget roughly $10–30/year until you have a registrar quote. The scenario monitoring/CI lines are $0–30/month allowances; retention-heavy logs, macOS builds and external uptime checks can require more.
  • Knowledge upkeep: budget 2–8 hours/month to review answers, update articles and evaluate regressions. At $50/hour that is $100–400. This also exists when you buy a support tool; do not count it as a DIY-only disadvantage.
  • Human support: both options still need people to answer exceptions. Use your actual staffing budget on both sides of the comparison. An AI feature is not a commitment to answer everything correctly.
  • Security, privacy and recovery: initial internal checks are in the work packages. External penetration testing, specialist advice and contractual requirements need scoped quotes; there is no honest universal price. Deletion must reach files, embeddings and backups according to your retention design.

If you want to sell your build to other companies, add subscription billing, taxes, account provisioning, customer migrations, abuse management and support for the support tool. The estimates here do not include launching that second business.

When building is the right call.

Build when messaging differentiates the product: specialized collaboration, unusual data flows, deep proprietary workflows, or requirements a hosted vendor cannot meet. Build when you want to learn distributed product engineering. Build when you have an engineer who will remain responsible for it and the narrowed scope makes the economics work.

Buying is more attractive when the inbox is supporting the business rather than being the business. Running three SaaS apps, two Shopify apps and a weekend project creates a strong incentive to share the support layer while spending engineering time on the products themselves.

Frequently asked questions

Can I vibe code a live chat in a weekend?

A prototype or deliberately small internal tool, yes. A support suite with reliable email, native operator apps, project permissions, a help center and AI handoff is a different scope; use the work-package estimate rather than a demo’s completion time.

Can I run my own live chat for free?

A small web-only experiment can fit free allowances if its usage and daily peaks stay within their limits. Production email volume, paid AI, native distribution and your time can introduce costs even when the socket layer is free.

Is the live map expensive?

Not necessarily: approximate edge geolocation and a map within its free allowance can add no service charge. Presence tracking, clustering, privacy controls and avoiding repeated lookups are the parts you still implement.

What is usually the biggest cost?

For low-volume DIY support, it is often the time needed to build, test and maintain the complete workflow. At higher AI volume, model calls can dominate the service bill; the examples separate those two kinds of cost.
